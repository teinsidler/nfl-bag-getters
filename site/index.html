      How the numbers are counted
      • Each season counts under the latest contract the quarterback signed on or before that season, other than practice-squad deals. Drafted and undrafted-free-agent deals are rookie deals. An extension therefore counts from the year it was signed, including any seasons left on the rookie deal.
      • Cash paid is the season's cash from Over The Cap plus cash paid out as dead money after a release or trade. Cap cost is the season's cap hit plus dead cap charged to the team.
      • 2026 cap dollars multiply each year's money by how much the salary cap has grown since then, using Over The Cap's own inflation factors.
      • Share of team cap adds up each season's cap hit as a percentage of the cap, so three seasons at 10% count as 30%.
      • Production comes from nflverse and starts in 1999. A paid season with no stats counts as zero production. EPA (expected points added) adds passing and rushing EPA.
      • Wins and losses are the quarterback's record as the starter. nflverse credits a game to the quarterback who took most of the snaps, so a starter pulled early in a rest game loses that start. Regular-season and playoff wins are counted separately; "Games counted" picks which one wins, WPA and turnovers use. Other production stats are regular season only.
      • WPA (win probability added) sums how much each of a quarterback's dropbacks and designed runs changed his team's chance of winning, from nflverse's win-probability model. Turnovers are interceptions plus fumbles lost. For turnovers, more money per turnover is better, so the biggest bag getters are the ones paid least per turnover.
      • "Seasons as primary starter" counts seasons in your range where the quarterback made the most starts for his team. The Super Bowl filter also looks only at your range of seasons. Both narrow every ranking, list and chart in the quarterback view.
      • On the team page, "How he arrived" is decided at the start of each stint with a team: drafted or rookie deal here, signed as a free agent (including waiver claims), or traded for (the contract before his first one with the team ended in a trade). Dead cap is split by how the contract ended: traded away, or released, retired or void years accelerating.
      • Team page roles: the primary starter made the most starts for that team that season, a spot starter made at least one, a backup played without starting, and "didn't play" appeared in no games. Dead cap is money charged for a quarterback no longer on that deal. A season's pay sits with the team Over The Cap lists for that season, so a mid-season trade counts under one team.
      • Quarterbacks without a season-by-season pay history on Over The Cap (mostly short-stint backups) are not ranked. The 2026 season is in progress, so its full-year pay sits beside a partial season of stats.
